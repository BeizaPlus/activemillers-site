Thrombus Formation, Propagation & Resolution

Endothelial injury exposes collagen; platelets adhere and aggregate. Thrombin converts fibrinogen to fibrin, and the resulting mesh passively traps red cells — the early thrombus. Left untreated, it propagates by successive layers (lines of Zahn), progressively narrowing the vessel. Thrombolytic drugs (streptokinase, alteplase) activate plasmin, which cuts the fibrin scaffold — once it's gone, the trapped cells have nothing left to hold them, and the vessel can reopen.
