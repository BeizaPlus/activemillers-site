# Natural Spoken Voice — Style Reference

Running collection of natural, spoken-language patterns to write case study copy toward.
Drop new source clips/notes at the bottom under "Log." Extract the *pattern*, not the source
material itself: never paste full transcripts here, just the technique and a short illustrative
snippet.

Goal: case study prose that reads like someone explaining something to a smart colleague,
not a textbook paragraph. This is the same register used in the Fat Necrosis rewrite.

## Core patterns to imitate

- **Contractions everywhere.** "That's," "here's," "you're," "doesn't." Never "that is," "it is."
- **Short declarative fragments mixed into longer sentences.** One-line punches after a longer
  explanatory run. "No lipase. No pancreas. No saponification cascade at all."
- **Direct address / second person.** "Here's why." "So how do you tell them apart? Clinically."
  Talk to the reader, not about the topic in the abstract.
- **Rhetorical question, then immediate answer.** Ask the question the reader is already
  thinking, answer it in the next sentence or fragment. Don't let it hang.
- **"So" / "Now" / "Here's the thing" as sentence openers.** Natural spoken connectors that
  signal a turn in the explanation, used sparingly, not every sentence.
- **Concrete before abstract.** Lead with the specific example or image, only generalize after
  the reader has something to hold onto.
- **Repetition for emphasis, not variety.** Reusing a word or phrase across consecutive short
  sentences reads as spoken cadence, not as a writing flaw. "It's a torn cell membrane, not a
  digested one" style pairs.
- **Numbers stay exact, tone stays loose.** Precision on facts and figures never gets sacrificed
  for the conversational register — the two live independently.
- **No em dashes, ever.** Replace with period, comma, or colon. Site-wide rule, not just this
  format.
- **Avoid the explanation sandwich.** Don't bury the point in a parenthetical or triple-clause
  sentence. State it, then unpack it in the next sentence if needed.

## What NOT to import from marketing/sales voice

Source material for this style often comes from sales/marketing writing coaching (funnel
psychology, persuasion triggers, etc). Take the *cadence and rhythm* only. Do not import:
- Urgency/scarcity framing ("don't miss out," "act now")
- Sales CTAs or persuasion mechanics (choice-framing, proof-stacking) — a case study is
  teaching, not closing a sale
- Marketing jargon ("funnel," "conversion," "the ask")
- Any structure implying the reader should buy something

## Quick self-check before shipping copy

- Read a paragraph out loud. Where you stumble, that's friction — rewrite it.
- Could this sentence be said out loud in conversation without sounding stilted? If not, cut it down.
- Are there two clauses doing one clause's job? Split it, or cut the weaker half.
- Is there an em dash hiding anywhere? Replace it.
